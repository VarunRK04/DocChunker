ABSTRACT 
With the growing number of pet owners and increasing concern for animal 
welfare, the need for intelligent and accessible pet healthcare tools is more urgent 
than ever. This project presents a Flutter-based mobile application that uses AI to 
detect and manage skin conditions in dogs, drawing on CNN and TensorFlow Lite 
for real-time image analysis and diagnosis. Developed in Android Studio, the app
provides instant feedback and care suggestions, enhancing early detection of 
dermatological issues. Firebase integration enables secure user authentication, 
real-time data storage, and cloud-based tracking of medical histories, supporting 
remote and urban users alike. The app also features appointment scheduling, 
reminders, and tools for veterinarians to host health events and outreach 
programs. By combining mobile technology, AI, and cloud services, this solution
offers a practical, proactive approach to improving animal health and veterinary 
engagement. 
 
 
 
 Keywords: pet healthcare, mobile application, Flutter, Dart, TensorFlow Lite, 
CNN, Firebase, Android Studio, real-time image capture, dog skin condition 
detection, AI diagnosis, veterinary scheduling, animal welfare.

1.1 ABOUT THE DOMAIN 
 
The Pet Healthcare domain is rapidly advancing, focusing on smart, accessible 
solutions to improve the well-being of companion animals. With growing pet 
ownership, there's a rising need for tools that enable early diagnosis and easy 
access to veterinary care. Pet Pulse meets this demand through an AI-powered 
mobile app that uses TensorFlow Lite to detect dog skin conditions via real-time
image analysis and provide care suggestions, drawing on proven techniques in 
veterinary image-based diagnostics using CNN and lightweight deployment 
platforms [2][4]. It also includes features like vet appointment booking and event 
management, enhancing convenience for pet owners and professionals alike, 
similar to mobile systems developed for preliminary pet disease advice and 
offline accessibility [3]. By combining AI with mobile technology, Pet Pulse
supports proactive pet care and strengthens the connection between pets, owners, 
and veterinarians, extending successful methodologies previously demonstrated 
in related agricultural and veterinary applications [1][2][4]. 
 
1.1.1 DOMAIN CHALLENGES 
The pet healthcare domain faces several challenges that affect the development 
of intelligent solutions like Pet Pulse. A major issue is the lack of standardized, 
diverse datasets for animal health, especially for skin conditions, which limits
model training and generalization—a concern also highlighted in previous 
studies on canine dermatological classification using annotated image datasets 
[2][4]. Unlike human healthcare, veterinary medicine has limited public imaging 
databases, making it difficult to train accurate, adaptable AI models. Data 
collection also depends heavily on expert veterinary input, which is time-
consuming and constrained by limited availability and resources, as noted in
mobile systems designed to support non-expert users in preliminary diagnosis 
[3]. Another challenge is the wide variety of dog breeds, where the same
2 
 
condition can appear differently depending on factors like breed, fur type, age, 
or lighting—issues that impact the consistency of image-based diagnostic tools 
and were accounted for using data augmentation in previous works [2][4]. 
Additionally, not all pet owners have access to advanced smartphones or stable 
internet, making it essential to develop lightweight AI models that run efficiently 
on basic devices, as demonstrated through real-time, mobile-compatible systems
using TensorFlow Lite [4]. Overcoming these hurdles requires close 
collaboration between developers, veterinarians, and pet owners to create 
solutions that are both clinically reliable and widely accessible. 
 
1.2 MOTIVATION 
The motivation behind PET PULSE: Detecting Dog Diseases with TFLite and 
Vet Consult Booking lies in the growing need for accessible, smart, and efficient 
solutions in pet healthcare. As pet ownership rises globally, especially among dog
owners, common health issues like skin conditions often go unnoticed or are 
treated late due to the inconvenience of traditional in-clinic visits. PET PULSE 
addresses this gap by using TensorFlow Lite for real-time, image-based analysis 
of skin problems via a smartphone, allowing for early detection and timely care, 
aligning with proven approaches in lightweight CNN-based disease detection on 
mobile platforms [2][4]. Beyond diagnostics, the app simplifies veterinary access
through an integrated appointment booking system, reducing delays and ensuring 
prompt treatment—an advancement supported by mobile applications designed 
for symptom-based diagnosis and remote usability [3]. Additionally, its event 
management feature allows veterinarians to organize and promote activities such 
as vaccination drives and health awareness camps, improving engagement and 
outreach. By combining AI-powered diagnostics, scheduling, and event
coordination, PET PULSE provides a comprehensive, user-friendly platform that 
connects pet owners with veterinary professionals, making pet healthcare more 
proactive, responsive, and accessible, in line with current research advocating the
3 
 
integration of AI and mobile technologies for veterinary care [2][3][4]. 
 
1.3 PROBLEM STATEMENT 
Pet owners often struggle with detecting and properly managing skin conditions 
in dogs due to a lack of accessible diagnostic tools and timely veterinary support. 
Traditional methods of diagnosis require in-person visits, which may lead to 
delays in treatment, especially in remote or underserved areas. Additionally,
there is limited integration between diagnostic support, appointment scheduling, 
and awareness of local pet care events, leading to inefficiencies in overall pet 
health management. There is a need for a comprehensive, user-friendly solution 
that leverages technology to assist in early diagnosis, streamline access to 
veterinary care, and keep pet owners informed about relevant healthcare 
opportunities.

   1.4 EXISTING SYSTEM 
 The existing system in healthcare commonly utilizes cloud-based technologies to 
deliver diagnostic services. Institutions employ solutions like Secure Multi-party 
Computation (SMDC) and Networked AI for Disease Diagnosis (NAIAD) to 
process patient data securely and enable remote diagnosis. While these 
technologies support scalable access and AI integration, they often face several 
drawbacks related to user experience, efficiency, and data privacy.
 Cloud-Based Solutions: These platforms host diagnostic models online, 
allowing patients or healthcare providers to access services from anywhere. 
They offer high scalability and convenient data access. However, these 
solutions are heavily dependent on internet connectivity. In areas with poor 
network infrastructure, service delivery slows down. Moreover, there's a risk 
of exposing sensitive data during transmission.
4 
 
 SMDC and NAIAD Techniques: SMDC ensures secure processing 
without revealing sensitive data, and NAIAD enables private AI-based 
diagnostics. While they are technically sound, both are difficult to implement 
in real-time scenarios. Their performance often struggles in resource-limited 
devices. Additionally, the learning curve and technical complexity reduce their 
accessibility to general users. 
 
 AI Integration: Artificial intelligence improves the accuracy of diagnosis
when used in cloud settings. However, transmitting data to cloud servers causes 
delays. The reliance on external processing slows down immediate responses. 
This is especially critical in emergency cases. Moreover, updates or model 
changes require centralized redeployment. 
 
DISADVANTAGES OF THE EXISTING SYSTEM 


 
Causes delays in real-time diagnostics due to latency. 
 
Complex interfaces limit usability for non-technical users.
1.5 PROPOSED SYSTEM  
The proposed system is a mobile application designed for pet healthcare, 
specifically to detect skin conditions in dogs. It uses AI with TensorFlow Lite 
and CNNs to analyze skin images in real-time. Unlike cloud-based platforms, 
this app works offline on the device, ensuring better speed, privacy, and 
accessibility. 
AI-POWERED SKIN CONDITION DIAGNOSIS: The proposed system 
utilizes a pre-trained Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model, optimized
with TensorFlow Lite (TFLite), to analyze user-uploaded images of dogs' skin.
5 
 
The model detects potential skin conditions, such as infections, allergies, or 
parasites, by examining visual features and providing a diagnosis based on these 
findings. By leveraging the power of CNN-TensorFlow, the system can deliver 
real-time, accurate results directly to users, enabling quick diagnosis and timely 
intervention. The integration of TFLite API ensures that the model runs 
efficiently on mobile devices without compromising performance.
REAL-TIME DATABASE AND AUTHENTICATION: To enhance the user 
experience, the system incorporates a real-time database that stores the uploaded 
images and diagnostic results, allowing users to view their past reports and track 
progress over time. Real-time authentication is integrated using Flutter 
Framework, ensuring secure login and access to the platform. This system 
ensures that users' data is protected while allowing for easy updates and changes
to user credentials. The use of Flutter ensures cross-platform compatibility and 
a smooth user experience on both iOS and Android devices.  
 
EFFICIENT AND DYNAMIC CONFIGURATION: The proposed system is 
designed to be efficient and user-friendly. The application provides a dynamic 
configuration that allows for easy updates and adjustments to the diagnostic 
models, making it adaptable to new skin conditions or improvements in AI 
models.

2.1 LITERATURE REVIEW 
 
[1] Leaf Tar Spot Detection Using RGB Images (2022)  
 
 Sriram Baireddy, Da-Young Lee, Carlos Gongora-Canul, Christian D. 
Cruz, Edward J. Delp, This research proposes a deep learning-based method for 
detecting tar spot disease in corn leaves using Mask R-CNN. The disease, caused 
by the fungus Phyllachora maydis, leads to reduced crop yield and is traditionally 
detected through manual visual inspection, a time-consuming and inconsistent
process. To overcome this challenge, the authors devised an "automatic ground 
truthing" approach that utilizes traditional image analysis techniques, such as 
thresholding in HSV and LAB color spaces and connected component labeling, 
to generate segmentation masks without human annotation. These masks were 
then used to train a Mask R-CNN model. The model demonstrated significant 
accuracy, achieving an F1-score of 0.76 and reducing the average error in tar spot
count to 10.4 per image. This automated pipeline substantially decreases labeling 
time and allows for large-scale, high-throughput phenotyping. Their findings 
affirm the efficacy of Mask R-CNN in plant disease detection and provide a 
scalable solution for agricultural monitoring using RGB image data. 
 
[2] Paws and Pixels: Enhancing Canine Dermatology Through Image Analysis 
(2024) 
 
 Yashu, Ashish Garg, Vinay Kukreja, Shanmugasundaram Hariharan,
Prateek Srivastava, This paper addresses the challenges in veterinary 
dermatology by implementing a hybrid deep learning model that combines 
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) with a Random Forest classifier. The 
system is designed to detect and classify seven common canine dermatological 
diseases, including Canine Atopic Dermatitis, Pyoderma, Ringworm, Seborrhea, 
Demodicosis, Flea Infestation, and Autoimmune Skin Disorders. The model was
trained on a dataset of 4,053 annotated images and achieved an accuracy of
8 
 
79.02%. The CNN component efficiently extracts hierarchical spatial features, 
while the Random Forest provides robust classification by handling complex 
decision boundaries. The authors also used data augmentation and normalization 
to enhance model generalizability. Performance evaluation through precision, 
recall, and F1-scores indicated strong performance across most disease categories, 
with Flea Infestation and Canine Seborrhea being the most accurately classified.
The study contributes significantly to the field of animal health by offering a 
viable AI-assisted diagnostic tool for use in clinics and remote veterinary settings. 
 
[3] The Development of Application for Advice and Diagnosis of Diseases

Chalida Janthajirakowit, Srinuan Fongmanee, Sumran Chaikhamwang, 
This research focuses on designing and implementing a mobile application to 
provide advice and preliminary diagnosis of dog diseases. The application,
developed using the Ionic framework and powered by a decision tree model (ID3 
algorithm), enables users to identify possible diseases based on symptoms and age 
input. The application supports both online and offline functionality, increasing 
accessibility for pet owners in remote areas. The system offers detailed 
information on symptoms, causes, and preliminary treatments of common 
diseases and is specifically geared toward non-expert users. Evaluations by three
domain experts and twenty general users rated the application highly in terms of 
functionality, reliability, and usability. The research underlines the importance of 
such applications in improving early-stage diagnosis, supporting pet care, and 
reducing dependency on in-person veterinary visits for preliminary consultations.

[4] Canine Skin-Disease Detection Using CNN (2024) 
 
 B. Sai Harshitha, M. Vani Pujitha, B. Chetan Chandra, The paper presents a 
custom-designed Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model for the multi-class 
classification of five prominent canine skin diseases: Pyoderma, Leprosy, Flea 
Allergy, Ear Mites, and Ringworm. The model was trained on a dataset of 5,000 
images, incorporating rigorous preprocessing, including resizing, normalization,
and data augmentation. The CNN architecture consists of multiple convolutional 
layers followed by max pooling and fully connected layers, with dropout 
regularization to prevent overfitting. The proposed model achieved a 
classification accuracy of 89.7%, significantly outperforming traditional models 
such as SVM and even some generic CNN architectures like VGG-16 in terms of 
generalizability. The use of softmax in the output layer enabled effective multi-
class prediction. The study not only demonstrates the potential of deep learning 
in veterinary dermatology but also highlights the importance of domain-specific 
CNN architectures tailored to the nuances of medical image classification in 
animals. 
 
[5] Validation of a Wearable System for Respiratory Rate Monitoring in 
Dogs (2024) 
Alessandra Angelucci 1 , (Member, Ieee), Francesco Birettoni 2 , Antonello 
Bufalari2 , And Andrea Aliverti 1, One of the most relevant physiological
parameters in dogs is respiratory rate (RR). The aim of this paper is to present a 
novel wearable system that allows to accurately estimate RR in dogs, and to 
compare it to a gold standard in static conditions. Data from 12 dogs were 
acquired while the animals were anesthetized and attached to a vital signs monitor. 
The experimental setup consisted of three Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) 
applied on the dog, and a video camera filming the RR value shown on the
monitor. The range of RR values analyzed in the study is 0 to 29 breaths per 
minute, read by the vital signs monitor. The mean RMSE for the data acquisitions
10 
 
is 1.68 breaths per minute. The values of the filtering parameters that allow to 
obtain the best performance depend on the specific acquisition. This result 
demonstrates that adaptive filtering is a viable method for the application. Future 
developments include tests on a larger dataset, and trials on dogs in unconstrained 
environments and during movement. 
 
 
[6] Non-Contact Vital States Identification of Trapped Living Bodies Using 
Ultra-Wideband Bio-Radar (2021)
Ultra-Wideband Bio-Radar (2021) 
 
Yangyang Ma, Pengfei Wang, Huijun Xue, Fulai Liang, Fugui Qi, (Student 
Member, Ieee), Hao Lv, Xiao Yu, Jianqi Wang, And Yang Zhang, Identifying 
the vital states of trapped survivors during post-disaster rescue missions can result 
in improved rescue strategies and provide injury pre-diagnosis information. The 
most effective rescue method is the use of bio-radar based non-contact
measurements. Presently, bio-radar techniques focus on detecting and locating. 
Herein, a method to identify vital states with an ultra-wideband bio-radar is 
proposed, while simulating a trapped condition with Beagle dogs. This 
investigation revealed three vital stages under the trapped condition: normal, 
transitioning, and agonal stages. Upon entering the transitioning stage, the 
heartrates were apparently high, and the respiratory rates increased sharply. The
temperatures dropped rapidly once passing this stage. In particular, the respiratory 
waveforms from the bio-radar frequently change from a normal sine like curve to 
an ‘‘M’’ like curve within the transitioning stage. The accurate beginning and 
ending of the transitioning stage are defined by a newly proposed indicator of 
relative occurrence frequency. Pathological observations indicated that the 
fragmentation of lamellar bodies within type II alveolar cells caused the
insufficiency of the lung surfactant, and further resulted in the occurrence of the

5.1 DATASETS EXPLANATION  
5.1.1 SIGCOMP 2009 Dataset  
 
The SIGCOMP 2009 
 The dataset was introduced for the Signature Verification Competition 2009 
to advance research in the field of offline signature verification. It provides a 
standardized set of signature samples to evaluate the performance of various 
algorithms. 
 
 This dataset contains genuine and forged signatures from multiple 
individuals. Each participant contributed several genuine signatures, along
with a set of skilled forgeries. The signature images are typically available in 
TIFF or PNG format and are clearly labeled as either genuine or forged.  
 
With a total of 1,564 samples, this dataset serves as a robust benchmark for 
assessing signature verification systems. It has been widely used to train and 
test models such as Support Vector Machines (SVMs), Neural Networks, and 
more advanced deep learning techniques. Its rich variability and labeled data
make it a valuable resource for testing the reliability of automated signature 
verification systems.  
 
5.1.2 CEDER DATASET  
 
The CEDER (Cedar Signature) Dataset is another key resource in offline 
signature verification research. It consists of handwritten signatures from a 
wide range of individuals, including both genuine and forged samples.
30 
 
 
Primarily used for developing and evaluating biometric authentication 
systems, this dataset supports a variety of applications such as banking, legal 
documentation, and identity verification. The images are generally stored in 
PNG or JPEG format. Standard preprocessing techniques—such as noise 
removal, image enhancement, and feature extraction—are often applied 
before feeding them into machine learning models. 
 
The CEDER dataset is widely employed in experiments involving
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Autoencoders, and other deep 
learning methods, and provides ample data to test the robustness and 
generalization capability of such systems.
